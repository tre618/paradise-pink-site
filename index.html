Our Story

Built in Honor of DeGeta Cole

DeGeta Cole was a Stage 1 breast cancer survivor. After completing additional chemotherapy intended to help reduce the risk of recurrence, she later contracted COVID-19 and passed away in 2020. Her son, D’Antré Cole, created Paradise Pink to honor her life and carry her legacy forward through music, culture, community, and breast cancer awareness.

D’Antré Cole is a filmmaker and creative entrepreneur. He now uses that storytelling background to create experiences that bring people together around breast cancer awareness: community events, music, culture, and conversations that make people feel welcome and informed.

Paradise Pink is a music, culture, and events brand built with purpose.

Created by D'Antré Cole in honor of his mother, DeGeta Cole, Paradise Pink was born from love, loss, and the desire to transform heartbreak into hope. Pink was DeGeta's favorite color, and it lives at the heart of everything Paradise Pink represents.

DeGeta and her husband, Rodney Cole, were incredible parents who believed in giving, loving, and serving others. Their home was always open — not only to their own children, but to other kids who needed guidance, support, or simply a place where they felt loved. They raised their family with compassion, generosity, and a deep sense of community, values that continue to inspire everything Paradise Pink stands for today.

After surviving Stage 1 breast cancer, DeGeta completed additional chemotherapy to help reduce the risk of recurrence. Even through her treatments, she remained hopeful and focused on staying healthy because she and Rodney had planned the trip of a lifetime to Paris in 2021. She later contracted COVID-19, and that trip never came. DeGeta passed away on December 31, 2020, leaving behind a family that was completely devastated.

Just weeks earlier, she had celebrated her birthday surrounded by D'Antré and their family. None of them realized it would be the last birthday they would ever share together.

DeGeta 1955 – 2020

Determined to honor his mother's legacy, D'Antré poured his grief into creation instead of giving up. In just four months, he released six films while building an ecosystem of companies, creative projects, events, and communities designed to bring people together. Of everything he has created, Paradise Pink is the most personal. It is his greatest tribute to the woman who taught him the importance of love, kindness, and making people feel like they belong.

Paradise Pink brings together music, community, fashion, culture, and impact in every event we create. Every guest is invited to wear pink — not simply as a color, but as a symbol of unity, remembrance, strength, hope, and love.

In partnership with a major breast cancer organization, a portion of every ticket sold directly supports the fight against breast cancer. Every ticket, every sponsor, every performance, and every shared moment helps move that mission forward.

Paradise Pink is for the survivors. For the families. For the people we've lost. For those still fighting. And for everyone searching for a place where they are welcomed, celebrated, and never alone.

Paradise Pink is more than any single event. It is a movement — built from love, powered by community, and created to honor DeGeta Cole's legacy while bringing hope to families for generations to come.

Breast Cancer Awareness

Know Your Body. Protect Your Future.

Awareness is year-round. Early detection saves lives, and knowing what to look for, when to get screened, and where to go is the first step. This is the information DeGeta's family wishes more people had.

Know Your Normal

Pay attention to how your breasts normally look and feel. A new lump, skin dimpling, nipple changes, or unusual discharge is worth a call to a health care professional.

Make Screening a Priority

Mammograms can find breast cancer before symptoms appear. Ask your clinician when to start and how often to screen based on your age and risk. Many guidelines begin at age 40.

Know Your Risk

Share your family history and any inherited cancer risk with your clinician. A family history can change when and how you should be screened.

Most breast changes are not cancer. New changes still deserve attention.

Lower Your Risk

No habit can guarantee prevention, but these can help reduce risk.

  • 1
    Stay physically activeRegular movement is linked to a lower risk of breast cancer.
  • 2
    Maintain a healthy weightEspecially after menopause, excess weight raises risk.
  • 3
    Limit alcoholThe less you drink, the lower the risk. No amount is risk free.
  • 4
    Don't smokeQuitting benefits your overall health and lowers cancer risk.
  • 5
    Breastfeed if you canBreastfeeding may slightly lower risk for some women.
  • 6
    Don't skip screeningIt does not prevent cancer, but it finds it early when treatment works best.

Signs to Watch For

See a health care professional if you notice any of these.

  • •
    A new lump or thickeningIn the breast or underarm area.
  • •
    Skin changesDimpling, puckering, redness, or an orange-peel texture.
  • •
    Nipple changesTurning inward, new pain, or unusual discharge.
  • •
    Change in size or shapeSwelling or shrinking of all or part of a breast.

Men can get breast cancer too. Anyone who notices these changes should get checked.

Beyond the Myths

Breast cancer facts worth sharing.

MYTH

Only women get breast cancer.

FACT

Men can get breast cancer too.

MYTH

No family history means no risk.

FACT

Most women diagnosed have no known family history.

MYTH

Breast cancer always causes symptoms.

FACT

Early breast cancer often has no symptoms.
